What do a scene from _Silicon Valley_, a typeface shaped like sausages, and a bottle of mustard have in common? They're all part of the origin story of Notdog, one of Font Awesome's small-batch Pro+ icon packs. It's glossy, it's gooey, and it is very much not a hot dog.

On this episode of Podcast Awesome, Matt sits down with Font Awesome designers Noah Jacobus and Jory Raphael to talk about how a challenge from Dave Gandy ("can we make an icon pack that looks like this?") turned into one of the first proofs that Pro+ packs could work at all. Noah explains why one simple rule about shine shaped every icon in the set, why his early emoji faces looked like dino nuggets, and how a pack inspired by tubes ended up looking like condiments. Plus: the actor who plays Milton in the Font Awesome marketing videos eats a lot of hot dogs on set, and we settle nothing about which regional hot dog is best.

**What We Cover**

-   How a _Silicon Valley_ gag and the Frankfurter typeface combined into Notdog
-   Why every icon needed room for a highlight, and how that set the smallest possible shape
-   The shift from tube-shaped strokes to blobby, condiment-style forms
-   How Noah builds a test set of about 10 icons before committing to all 200
-   Why Pro+ packs trade some legibility for personality, like a headline typeface
-   The faces that fought back, and why the Duotone style is the one to use
-   Notdog's starring role in the Font Awesome 7 video
-   Chicago dogs, Seattle dogs, and the legendary $1.50 Costco hot dog
