You've seen it a thousand times: you're two minutes late for a video conference call, you throw on a shirt with buttons (It's okay that you're still wearing jammie pants. Don't worry, nobody can see), and right when you need your computer most, that colorful spinning ball shows up to ruin your day. It's called a lot of things, but most of us just call it a curse word.

Today we trace the beach ball of death back to its actual origin story, which involves a $10,000 computer that flopped commercially, Steve Jobs in exile, a forgotten storage format, and Susan Kare's sketchbook. Turns out the most hated icon in computing has a surprisingly great pedigree.

We also get into why the beach ball exists at all (psst: it's your OS parenting a misbehaving app), how it could've been a spinning slice of pizza, and why Apple Silicon might be quietly killing it off for good.

**  
What We Cover**

-   How much of life is spent waiting on machines, and why the frustration doesn't stop when you sit down at your computer
-   The Xerox PARC origins of the "wait" cursor and Susan Kare's contributions at Apple
-   Windows 95 and the golden age of customizable cursors (RIP, dancing banana)
-   The lineage from hourglass to wristwatch to spinning disk to beach ball to flat ring
-   How NeXT, the company Steve Jobs founded after leaving Apple, gave us the beach ball's true ancestor
-   Why Apple's 1996 acquisition of NeXT brought the spinning cursor along with it
-   How Aqua and Mac OS X turned a monochrome disk icon into a glossy rainbow ball
-   The many names for the beach ball, including the delightfully specific "bun of death"
-   Why the beach ball is becoming rarer on modern Macs, and what that means for a whole generation of users
