Picture this serious, sterile scene: scientists in lab coats, pocket protectors, carrying clipboards, and nobody’s making eye contact. Nobody would guess that this backdrop would produce some of the best internet lore.

In this episode, we dig into the myth that the 404 error page came from "Room 404" at CERN, track down what a co-creator of the web actually had to say about it, and land on the real, much more boring origin. Along the way we detour into the HTTP status code that was invented as an April Fools' joke, then defended by the developer community hard enough that it got its own RFC. We close it out with a tour of the internet's best 404 pages, including a deep dive into Font Awesome's own, with Jory Raphael.

  

**What We Cover**

-   The Room 404 myth: how it started, why it spread, and why Robert Cailliau, one of the actual creators of the web, wants nothing to do with it
-   The real, much less exciting origin of the 404 status code
-   Why HTTP works like a librarian with no short-term memory
-   Larry Masinter's HTCPCP proposal and the accidental birth of the 418 "I'm a teapot" code
-   How the dev community fought to save 418 when the IETF tried to reclaim it
-   A tour of the internet's best 404 pages: GitHub, Pixar, Lego, and Figma
-   Behind the scenes of Font Awesome's own 404 pages with Jory Raphael, including the Ghostbusters, Planes, Trains and Automobiles, Star Wars, and Mario references hiding in them
-   The Easter egg hiding on the Font Awesome icons page (and another one on Blog Awesome)
