What would you change if you could start over? (We’re talking about icons btw, not life. That’s a different podcast.) Jory Raphael has spent years designing Font Awesome. He sat down with a blank 16px grid, everything he’s learned since, and one question: if we built Font Awesome today, what choices would we make?

The answer became Utility, one of our small batch icon packs for Pro+ subscribers. It uses true 2px strokes, which forces every icon down to its essentials. It’s built to be neutral, the kind of set that fades into a dashboard and lets you get your work done. The personality hides in the details: square end caps, rounded outer corners, and sharp inner corners.

Jory and Noah Jacobus join Matt to talk about the rules that kept Utility consistent, when they broke those rules, and a highlighter icon that refused to cooperate. They also share a first look at a much bigger version of the pack that’s on the way. And yes, we fix an icon live during the recording.

🗒️ What We Cover  
📏 Why Utility uses a true 2px stroke when Font Awesome Regular is 1.5px  
⚖️ How Jory mixed round and sharp corners and end caps, and why that makes the set feel neutral  
🧪 How the small batch packs get started, including the “could I build 300 of these?” test  
📐 Writing a rules page for an icon set, and when to break those rules  
✒️ Interior stroke weights and preventing “bloom” in filled styles and cutouts  
🔦 The highlighter problem, and what the wrench icon taught Jory  
🛠️ A first look at the next version of Utility: about 140 new icons, Light, Regular, and Black weights, and a new Duotone style  
🔄 Experimental serif, fully sharp, and rounded versions  
📊 Where Utility fits best: dashboards, productivity apps, and data-dense UI
