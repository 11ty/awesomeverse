What happens when you take icons built to scale cleanly at any size and deliberately make them look like they were drawn on a Game Boy? You get Pixel, Font Awesome’s retro video game–inspired Pro+ icon pack.

On this episode of Podcast Awesome, Matt sits down with Font Awesome designers Noah Jacobus and Jory Raphael to dig into how Pixel came together. They cover the Game Boy, Panic’s little yellow Playdate, a tip of the hat to original Mac icon designer Susan Kare, and the surprisingly sneaky math behind faking pixels with vectors. Bonus: Jory admits to lying about file sizes in real time.

This one gets visual, with grids, key shapes, and pixel cheats on screen, so it’s worth watching on YouTube instead of just listening.

🗒️ What We Cover  
🕹️ Why the original Game Boy shaped Pixel’s look, right down to that shadowy old display  
🍌 The Playdate, Panic’s tiny banana-colored handheld, as a daily dose of pixel art inspiration  
🖥️ Easter eggs on the Pixel page: a Playdate settings screen and a classic Mac System 7 control panel  
📱 How Font Awesome took off by scaling cleanly on high-density screens, and how Pixel runs the opposite direction  
💀 “Pixel cheating”: why the skull’s eyes sit on a different grid than its outline  
🔲 Landing on a 24-pixel grid after three or four rounds of test sets  
✏️ Why removing a single pixel from a corner is a huge design decision  
📐 Why vector “pixels” actually make bigger files than smooth curves  
🎸 Creative constraints, Jack White, and four-track recorders  
👾 A Space Invaders prototype built from Pixel icons, and a possible Space Awesome cameo
